History of SCREAMING FIST

The SCREAMING FIST Special Operations Force was activated in 2013 by new elected President Charles Miers. Initially brought into existence specifically to counter the newly formed NSU “Black Hand”, the Screaming Fist would prove to be an invaluable resource in numerous different political situations. From Central America to the frozen wastes of Siberia, the Screaming Fists risked and sometimes gave up their lives in order to protect democracy from the spreading Soviet threat.

Like the Special Ops forces brought into existence before it, SF’s main operational purpose was to reinforce, supplement, augment, and support the conventional forces during, before, and after any given conflict. The SF would be deployed prior to conventional forces in order to prepare and organize the field for positioning of combat troops, demonstrate support to allies, and a host of other prehositlity missions. Once a conflict has started, the SF would become involved in economy-of-force operations, generating strategic advantages which are unequal to the resources they represent. The SF could located, seize, or destroy certain key enemy targets, with a minimal risk to the force in general. They could obtain enemy intelligence, disorganize, disrupt, and demoralize enemy troops, or divert essential resources.

While SF’s main purpose may have been support, it also held more significant and autonomic importance. For example, the SF could often expand the options of domestic organizations such as the National Command Authority in cases of terrorism, insurgency, subversion, or sabotage where the use of a large conventional force may not be prudent.

The scope of this new Special Ops Force expanded greater than either Miers or its original organizers probably could have imagined. During the first several years of existence, it operated out of Fort Melville, NM, a secluded and hidden base deep in the New Mexico Desert, several miles from Los Alamos (this location would later prove key to the future of the SF). Selecting military members who showed exceptional abilities in several key areas, SF recruits were put through some of the most rigorous training and cross-training in the world by the very finest professional military operators.

Then, in 2016, the Honduran Conflict begins. The Miers administration attempts to help the democratic forces of South America through economic means; this, however, proves futile since the Soviets equally back the Communist forces. As an effort to avoid sending in conventional forces, Miers sends in the Screaming Fist, to participate in both Direct Action (which involved short, offensive strikes such as raids, sabotage, and ambushes) and Foreign Internal Defense (which involved the training and advising of South American friendlies) capacities.

During this campaign, the Technical Division at Fort Melville begins to acquire both funding and technology from the base at Los Alamos as the result of a memo from Miers himself requesting that the Screaming Fist program take top military priority. Between the two divisions, state of the art firepower and military vehicles begin to be developed and field-tested. This “partnership” between the two research facilities results in a major leap in military technology. Much of this technology is tested by operatives in the South American Campaign.

Despite such advantages, the Screaming Fist operatives find themselves highly outnumbered, and casualties are high. The continued disorganization and in-fighting within the Democratic factions, coupled with the harsh terrain and climate result in a very bloody and costly campaign for the Americans. After only six months in the field, the Screaming Fist pull out, with a cumulative casualty rate of almost 75% and three South American countries lost to the Soviets.

However, regardless of this “defeat”, the Screaming Fist program continues. The Honduran veterans return to Fort Melville to continue training with the newly developed technology. High ranking officers continue to actively recruit single, highly talented officers, technicians, and soldiers from all segments of the military. The Honduran Campaign is not seen as a defeat but rather as where the SF “broke our cherry, had our first shot of Jack, and got our first scar” (as so aptly put by Sgt. Dwayne Burke, Honduran Campaign Vet).

By 2017, the SF program had bypassed its previous numbers and once again stood out as the country’s largest and best trained Special Forces Unit (although their existence remained secret to the public for until 2024). During this highly crucial year in the “New Cold War”, the SF were assigned to perform perhaps the most dangerous and important mission in Special Forces history: an extraction of a Russian scientist named Vlad Lenov, who held the key to the balance of power between The NSU and the USA.
